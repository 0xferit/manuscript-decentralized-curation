# Color Effects

**Source:** [Color & Contrast](https://colorandcontrast.com/) — A comprehensive guide to color for UI designers

---

## Simultaneous contrast

Simultaneous contrast is an effect where the perceived hue, saturation, or lightness of a color is influenced by adjacent colors. A color can appear different depending on the surrounding palette, even when the color itself does not change.

This effect is not perceived the same way by everyone, and some people may not experience it for certain color combinations. Do not rely on simultaneous contrast alone as a mechanism for distinguishing UI elements.

## Chromostereopsis

Chromostereopsis occurs because different wavelengths of light refract through a lens at different angles. Their focal points can land at different depths on the retina, creating an impression of depth between colors.

Effects of chromostereopsis may be exaggerated by corrective lenses for some viewers, which can make certain color pairings feel visually unstable or harder to focus on.

## Successive contrast

Successive contrast is an illusion where the appearance of a color is affected by previously viewed colors. It results from afterimages caused by chromatic adaptation.

Component state changes or interactions that involve large color shifts can create undesirable results from successive contrast. In extreme cases, this can reduce legibility.

Dramatic successive-contrast effects can also be detrimental to some people and may contribute to migraines or other symptoms. People may not experience these effects equally.

## Spatial color mixing

All colors on digital displays are perceived through spatial color mixing of pixel-level red, green, and blue signals. For example, a teal color can appear from the spatial mixing of white, green, and blue elements at small scales.

## Purkinje effect

> Peak wavelength sensitivity of photoreceptors shifts toward blue in low light during dark adaptation. As light levels decrease, reds appear relatively darker than other colors.
