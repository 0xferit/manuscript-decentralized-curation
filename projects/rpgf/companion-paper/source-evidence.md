## P01. Accuracy: publication, pinned costs, outcomes and challenge grounds

Source P, lines 218–232.

Raw-excerpt SHA-256: `51b45322c62e2e480d5610e360f77ddd3c839746bffe2c97fa6269d7fea25145`.

Displayed-excerpt SHA-256: `51b45322c62e2e480d5610e360f77ddd3c839746bffe2c97fa6269d7fea25145`.

> 1. An author posts a claim blob and locks a bond. Contracts enforce minimal structural field presence at submission (non-empty CIDs, valid version pointers).
> 2. The item enters `Live` status immediately and remains challengeable while bonded. Authors may adjust their bond while live, subject to a pool-scoped grace period; the old bond rate continues for confidence accrual and challenge pricing until the adjustment takes effect.
> 3. A challenger may file one active challenge at a time against a specific proposition inside the item, with queued challenges allowed behind it. Challenges may also be filed against claims in `PendingEdit` or `WithdrawPending` states, targeting the last finalized revision.
> 4. The challenger pays a counter-stake (`max(pool minimum, bond/4)`), a challenge tax (a pool-configurable percentage of the pinned bond), and the external DDR fee. Each challenge is pinned to the challenged revision, manifest CID, and bond at filing time, so later edits or bond changes do not alter the dispute target or payout basis. For queued challenges, the counter-stake, tax, and DDR fee remain in escrow until activation; tax is credited to the pool budget only on activation.
> 5. The dispute goes to external DDR with a binary question: should the challenge succeed? Appeals, if any, are handled entirely by the external DDR. The protocol does not implement its own escalating-stakes appeal ladder or any protocol-native "lone expert versus herd" jackpot.
> 6. If the challenge succeeds, the item moves to `Closed` with adjudication outcome `Debunked`. The challenger receives the author's bond and their own counter-stake back; the challenge tax remains in the pool budget. If the challenge fails, the item returns to its pre-challenge operational state. The author keeps the bond; the challenger's counter-stake is split between author reward and pool reward budget at a pool-configurable ratio. The split ratio is a pool parameter, not a protocol constant: too much to the author weakens pool funding; too much to the pool weakens the author's incentive to defend claims. The DDR fee is a sunk challenger cost in both outcomes.
>
> Publication is optimistic: satisfying the structural transaction requirements and posting the bond does not certify a claim's truth, falsifiability or evidential support. Those semantic questions remain open to challenge rather than requiring prior approval. A successful factual-falsification or non-falsifiability challenge debunks the bonded item, forfeits its bond under the rule above and ends that item's beneficiary eligibility in applications that allocate funding. The outcome attaches to the item, not to its author, nominator or beneficiary as a person. The same participant may submit a fresh nomination with its own bond under the pool's ordinary submission rules; a prior debunking alone cannot bar it. The old item remains closed, and a new submission neither overturns that outcome nor inherits its score, funding entitlement or bond. Historical reputation records are distinct from this item-level adjudication and do not create a right to reject the fresh nomination solely because an earlier one was debunked.
>
> The protocol supports four typed challenge reasons, none of which is a separate final verdict. All yield `Debunked` on success:
>
> - `Debunking`: the challenged proposition is factually false.
> - `NonFalsifiable`: the challenged proposition fails to instantiate a falsifiable claim under the pool's template and evidence policy.
> - `ScopeViolation`: the claim does not belong in the pool's declared topic domain.
> - `TemplateViolation`: the claim passes on-chain structural checks but fails the pool's semantic template requirements (e.g., missing required fields or disallowed source classes). On-chain structural validation is enforced at submission; semantic template conformance is enforceable post-publication through this challenge reason.

## P02. Relevance: policy-relative weights and evidence readiness

Source P, lines 240–244.

Raw-excerpt SHA-256: `ee5cb4da3cb64d0228a437fec34343843543136bd2795e1eaee3009e74c647de`.

Displayed-excerpt SHA-256: `ee5cb4da3cb64d0228a437fec34343843543136bd2795e1eaee3009e74c647de`.

> Each pool creator defines the pool's own relevance rubric, with no protocol-wide default curation policy. Where an application allocates funding directly in proportion to curated relevance, the scores express relative funding weights, beyond an ordering of nominations. Scores of 0.75 and 0.25 carry a 3:1 funding-weight ratio before any binding request caps or other application factors. This ratio is an allocation choice under the pool's rubric, not a measured ratio of public benefit, causal impact or the benefit of an additional grant. The rubric should explain its intended numeric meaning. This interpretation concerns beneficiary funding; curator voting weights and appeal-backers' inverse-distance payouts retain their separate rules. It does not turn the news-feed ranking rule into a funding formula.
>
> For direct proportional funding, positive eligible weights divide the entire amount available for allocation. A sole eligible nomination with positive weight therefore receives that whole amount even when its score is low. A requested-weight cap, where used, limits its relative weight rather than placing an absolute ceiling on its payment. This is intended to create an opportunity for other eligible nominations to enter and compete for a share under the pool's ordinary admission rules; it does not establish that entry occurs, that competitors are independent or that allocation improves. Low positive scores alone do not withhold part of the distributable budget. Existing eligibility, holdback, zero-total-weight and funding-availability rules still apply; the rule does not spend reserved or unavailable funds.
>
> Nominations are responsible for having the material required by their pool's policy ready for relevance assessment. This does not introduce a separate evidence-approval or availability gate before drafting. Missing or inaccessible evidence can support a challenge where it establishes a violation of that policy; it does not by itself prove factual falsity or assign a zero relevance score. Absence of a successful challenge likewise does not establish that jurors received sufficient information or that the nomination is accurate. The relevance jury assesses the nomination under the pinned policy and the existing round rules; it does not certify the accuracy layer by completing a score.

## P03. Jury: deposits, drafting, private reports, quorum and dispersion

Source P, lines 246–272.

Raw-excerpt SHA-256: `b7ea8708a74a93e6cfc185d83d47c45b3651f28ece93dfe53d8bd684c9067cb9`.

Displayed-excerpt SHA-256: `a73be87ba57dd76247464b5e0f66ddbfb279e63dbff3e4c6e4b369be6ffa1799`.

> 1. Curators deposit a dedicated utility token into a pool contract to signal availability for drafting. Let $s_i$ denote curator $i$'s deposited utility-token balance, with both $s_i$ and the seat size $L$ measured in that token's smallest on-chain unit. Ordinary-round rules allow withdrawal of utility tokens outside active-round locks, subject to separately specified penalties. Jury service fees, appeal contributions and other payment flows use ether (ETH), accounted in wei, with the explicit utility-token transfer to successful leak reporters specified below. ETH appeal contributions have their own locks under Public appeal funding; they are not part of $s_i$ and do not reduce utility-token draft availability. Earlier jury service alone does not lock additional ETH. A successful leak reporter receives the confiscated utility tokens and the violator's ETH service fee for that round under the rule below.
> 2. Ordinary relevance rounds are scheduled for `Live` items at a pool-defined cadence, subject to the per-item filing-window and appeal hold in Public appeal funding. For each item in a pool, at most one ordinary relevance round may be unfinished at a time, across all content revisions. The single-round check, round creation and ordinary jury-budget reservation are atomic when scheduling succeeds. A competing scheduling attempt must defer without creating another round or reserving a second budget. Exclusivity covers scheduled preparation, drafting, commitments, reveals, the proof-opening window and scoring until a valid ruling is issued or the attempt is cancelled. Valid issuance opens its appeal hold in the same transition that completes the ordinary round, leaving no gap for another ordinary round. Cancellation releases ordinary-round exclusivity under the existing cancellation rules; a permitted replacement or retry must acquire it anew and satisfy the applicable cadence, funding and eligibility conditions. Reaching a later scheduled due time, publishing a new revision or using another scheduler does not bypass the guard. Other items and pools remain independent, and appeal juries retain their own shared-group and retry rules.
> 3. Each seat in the lottery corresponds to $L$ locked tokens (pool-configurable, $L > 0$). Only curators with $s_i \ge L$ are eligible; the draft weight $d_i$ is the integer number of full seat-tickets the curator holds, computed by rounding down:
>
> $$
> d_i = \lfloor s_i / L \rfloor
> $$
>
> 4. The protocol draws seats from the ticket pool. Each drawn ticket locks $L$ tokens from the corresponding curator. A curator may receive at most $d_i$ seats in a single round. Let $n_i$ denote the number of seats drawn for curator $i$. The curator's round weight $w_i$ equals their total locked tokens:
>
> $$
> w_i = n_i \cdot L
> $$
>
> Ticket eligibility alone does not guarantee that a ticket can be backed. The reference draft separately checks the curator's available deposit, excluding pre-existing locks for other rounds, and skips tickets it cannot fund. Existing collateral cannot back a second round; the blueprint specifies the bounded draft and its cancellation conditions.
>
> Locked tokens are simultaneously the curator's influence on the weighted mean and their maximum loss from ordinary coherence and non-participation penalties. The remainder ($s_i - n_i \cdot L$) stays in the pool contract and is not exposed to this round's coherence or non-participation penalties; other active-round locks and separately specified penalties still apply. A round lock remains active until it is released, including while it awaits appeal-chain finality under step 7. ETH voluntarily committed to an appeal is independently unavailable for withdrawal or reuse under Public appeal funding. Such a contribution does not encumber utility tokens; utility-token balances remain subject to separately specified staking penalties.
>
> 5. Each drafted curator commits and reveals a single relevance score in $[0,1]$, weighted by $w_i$. Multiple seats increase the curator's weight on that single score, not the number of independent votes.
>
> 6. A relevance round requires valid, timely reveals covering at least 80% of its originally drawn seat weight. Let $W_{\mathrm{draw}}$ be the sum of all curators' original round weights fixed when a nonempty draft succeeds, before any penalties, and let $W$ be the sum of those original weights belonging to valid revealers. Quorum holds exactly when $W_{\mathrm{draw}}>0$ and $5W\ge4W_{\mathrm{draw}}$. Since each drawn seat has the same fixed weight, a draft of $N>0$ seats requires at least $\lceil4N/5\rceil$ seats represented by valid reveals. This fixed rule applies to initial and appeal rounds, with no minimum number of distinct curators. One curator can meet quorum if its valid reveal represents at least 80% of the drawn weight. Later penalties or balance changes cannot reduce the denominator, and a fresh retry uses its own completed draw. This is a participation requirement, not a guarantee of independent or truthful judgments. A drafted round failing quorum completes the proof-opening window below before cancellation: no new score or coherence reward is produced, and any previous relevance score for the same pinned revision remains in force; it cannot substitute for an assessment of a newer revision. Drafted curators who fail to commit or reveal lose their locked tokens as non-participants; valid participants recover their remaining round locks after any applicable leak penalties. A successful leak reporter can still receive confiscated utility tokens under the rule below. This also applies when nobody reveals; an empty draft cannot satisfy quorum. Otherwise, the protocol computes the weighted mean $\mu$ and weighted standard deviation $\sigma$ over valid reveals only, with total weight $W>0$:
>
> $$
> \mu = \frac{1}{W}\sum_i w_i \, v_i, \qquad \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_i w_i (v_i - \mu)^2}{W}}.
> $$
>
> Let $\varepsilon_\sigma>0$ be the pool-configured dispersion floor. The mean is accepted as the round's relevance score whatever its dispersion. Step 7 floors the dispersion that scales its coherence band at $\varepsilon_\sigma$, so near-identical reports in the adjudicating round never face a vanishing band. The floor does not distinguish informed agreement from lazy convergence: a low-dispersion committee is penalized only when its reports lie outside the band around a different terminal adjudicated score.

## P04. Jury service fees and their earning boundary

Source P, lines 274–283.

Raw-excerpt SHA-256: `af2e5677cbcca7c610f57f4079f6bf075566e4bdc4e2174c8eff87da05a1d849`.

Displayed-excerpt SHA-256: `43a9ba6184a341508fa675a06bf8dabd4e76b0ee544775c2a30f889c5fd5fafe`.

> Jury compensation uses two independent asset ledgers. Let $N$ be the number of drawn seat-tickets and $p_{\mathrm{seat}}>0$ the configured ETH service budget per seat. The committed service budget is $R=Np_{\mathrm{seat}}$, in wei, for initial and appeal rounds alike. Ordinary rounds reserve ETH from their pool budget; appeal rounds use Public appeal funding. A fee is earned only when a valid ruling is issued. Each curator who submits a timely, valid reveal generates the normal gross ETH service fee for every originally drawn seat represented by that reveal, regardless of the score, coherence classification or dispersion. That fee belongs to the curator unless a proven leak redirects it to the successful reporter under the rule below. A curator representing several seats earns the corresponding multiple; a missing, late or invalid reveal generates no fee of its own. Let $N_{\mathrm{valid}}$ be the number of originally drawn seats represented by timely, valid reveals. For a valid issued ruling,
>
> $$
> \operatorname{fee}_i=n_i p_{\mathrm{seat}}\quad\text{for each timely, valid revealer},\qquad
> J=N_{\mathrm{valid}}p_{\mathrm{seat}}\le R.
> $$
>
> Other curators generate no normal ETH service fee of their own; a successful reporter can still receive a violator's redirected fee under the rule below. If the round fails quorum or otherwise produces no valid ruling, every service fee is zero, including for valid revealers. Unpaid seat fees are not redistributed to paid jurors. An ordinary round returns its unused pool reservation under the existing finalization or cancellation rule. A quorum-failed appeal attempt instead retains its reservations for the retry. When a valid appeal ruling issues, the pool-first fee accounting below releases unused pool reserves; unused contest reserves remain in the common pot until chain finality. Coherence affects the separate utility-token incentives, not the ETH fee, and no utility-token quantity is added to the ETH budget. The same valid-ruling issuance transition makes each earned ETH fee withdrawable immediately by its assigned recipient, after applying any proven-leak recipient override. Withdrawal does not wait for the appeal window, chain finality, another juror or a backer to claim. A later appeal that changes the score does not reduce or claw back this earned fee. Immediate withdrawability does not require an automatic transfer. The permissionless collection rule below applies to these earned ETH fees; the remaining transfer mechanics are not yet specified. The earned fee is charged once under the applicable funding rule, and an unclaimed fee remains committed to its assigned recipient rather than becoming reusable pool funds or part of the backers' settlement pot. Collecting it satisfies that entitlement without charging the funding source again.
>
> A timely, valid reveal establishes participation, not research effort or accuracy. Fixed ETH fees can make low-effort reporting profitable if expected utility-token losses are too small. Fee and stake calibration must therefore be assessed jointly, including low-value utility tokens and coordinated reports; this eligibility rule does not establish truthful incentives.

## P05. Utility-token penalties, redistribution and rounding

Source P, lines 287–321.

Raw-excerpt SHA-256: `b607c68a10f93333b45cb1323bb62ce43c07b782c04b5e097b9fc6071558fa66`.

Displayed-excerpt SHA-256: `bab7e8c45e491dee32d0a33e818e19edd6814c51fd053af17d02565b23491129`.

> Utility-token coherence rewards consist solely of redistributed distance-based incoherence penalties under step 7. There is no minted reward or separately funded base token reward. If no such tokens are slashed, the additional utility-token reward is zero, while the valid ruling can still earn its separately accounted ETH service fee. Claim 3 analyzes a single-asset reward model, not an established payoff of this two-asset design.
>
> 7. Coherence is graded against the outcome of the round's appeal chain, not the round alone. An ordinary ruling starts a chain, and each appeal ruling joins the chain of the ruling it references (Public appeal funding). Once the chain is final, its **terminal adjudicated ruling** is its last valid jury ruling. A score set by forfeiture can fix the terminal score for backer settlement, but it is a procedural funding outcome rather than a jury finding, so it never serves as the coherence reference; the terminal adjudicated ruling is then the last jury ruling before the forfeiture. Let $\mu^*$ and $\sigma^*$ be that ruling's weighted mean and dispersion, and let $\tilde\sigma^*=\max(\sigma^*,\varepsilon_\sigma)$. Let $v_i$ denote curator $i$'s revealed score and let $K$ denote the coherence-threshold multiplier ($K > 0$). Every committee in the chain, including the terminal one, is graded against this same reference. Curators inside the coherence band ($|v_i - \mu^*| \le K\tilde\sigma^*$) are not penalized. Curators outside the band lose a fraction of their locked tokens that scales linearly with distance:
>
> $$
> p_i = \min\!\Bigl(1,\;\max\!\bigl(0,\;\frac{|v_i - \mu^*|/\tilde\sigma^* - K}{K}\bigr)\Bigr)
> $$
>
> At the band boundary, the penalty is zero; at twice the boundary distance ($2K\tilde\sigma^*$ from $\mu^*$), the penalty is total loss of locked tokens. Since $w_i$ is measured in smallest on-chain units (integers), the slashed amount is rounded down:
>
> $$
> \delta_i = \lfloor p_i \cdot w_i \rfloor, \qquad q_i = w_i - \delta_i.
> $$
>
> The protocol slashes $\delta_i$ utility-token units and returns the remainder $q_i$. This graduated slashing replaces binary slashing: near-boundary deviations incur small losses, while extreme deviations incur total loss. Low dispersion grants no exemption: the floor keeps the band at least $K\varepsilon_\sigma$ wide around $\mu^*$, and reports outside it are penalized. Voluntary appeal contributions retain their separate lock under Public appeal funding. Failed commit or reveal participation remains subject to loss of the non-participant's locked tokens.
>
> Valid revealers' round locks stay locked until the chain reaches finality under Public appeal funding, and this step runs once for every committee in the chain at that event. Without an admitted appeal, a ruling becomes final when its admission window closes, so every valid round lock waits at least $\Delta_{\mathrm{admit}}$ after issuance. An appealed chain keeps the locks of all its committees until its last level settles, with no fixed bound because appeal depth is unlimited. A terminal exit that cancels the initial appeal process fixes the referenced ruling as the terminal adjudicated ruling at that cancellation. Locked collateral cannot back another round, so this hold reduces the stake a curator can offer to other drafts while the chain is open. Non-participation and leak penalties keep their own timing. An appeal ruling that moves the score therefore changes the settlement of earlier committees: a report coherent with its own round's mean is penalized when it lies outside the band around $\mu^*$, and a report its own round's band would have penalized is returned in full when it lies inside.
>
> For each committee in the chain, let $V$ be its valid revealers and $H=\{i\in V:p_i=0\}$ its coherent recipients. Only the actual integer distance-based penalties $S=\sum_{j\in V}\delta_j$ fund coherence rewards. For nonempty $H$, let $W_H=\sum_{j\in H}w_j$. The ideal additional utility-token entitlement is
>
> $$
> T_i^*=S\frac{w_i}{W_H}\quad(i\in H),\qquad T_i^*=0\quad(i\notin H).
> $$
>
> Each coherent curator receives the floor of its ideal entitlement in the utility token's smallest transferable unit. Count each curator once, using its entire original locked weight, rather than rounding separately for each represented seat:
>
> $$
> T_i=\left\lfloor\frac{S w_i}{W_H}\right\rfloor\quad(i\in H),\qquad
> T_i=0\quad(i\notin H),\qquad
> d_{\mathrm{token}}=S-\sum_{i\in H}T_i.
> $$
>
> When the committee's utility rewards are allocated at chain finality, credit $d_{\mathrm{token}}$ exactly once to that same pool's separate utility-token balance. A committee's penalties fund only that committee's coherent curators; no utility tokens move between levels. The conservation identity is $S=\sum_{i\in H}T_i+d_{\mathrm{token}}$, with $0\le d_{\mathrm{token}}\le |H|-1$ when $H$ is nonempty. Evaluate the product and division with exact floor semantics; intermediate overflow or truncation must not add further losses. Unlike the appeal-position bound, this bound depends on the number of coherent curators, not the proposal-grid size. A positive ideal reward may round to zero, and the pool may retain all of a sufficiently small $S$. For example, five smallest token units shared by two equally weighted coherent curators pay two units to each and leave one in the pool; three units shared by four equally weighted coherent curators pay zero to each and leave all three in the pool.
>
> These rewards are additional to returned stake $q_i$ and only redistribute tokens already confiscated. The retained balance does not enter the ETH service budget, convert to ETH or create a base subsidy for future coherence rewards. Allocated but unclaimed integer rewards remain assigned to their recipients and cannot be swept into the retained balance. If $S=0$, every $T_i=0$ and there is no remainder. If $H$ is empty, no denominator is evaluated and all of $S$ goes to the pool's distinct utility-token balance rather than its ETH service budget; the nonempty-set remainder bound does not apply. Non-participation penalties retain their separate pool destination and do not enter $S$. Leak penalties also do not enter $S$. A successful leak reporter receives all utility tokens actually confiscated through the violator's leak penalty, plus the entire normal gross ETH service fee associated with that violator in the affected round. This is an explicit exception to the general ETH payment rule: confiscated utility tokens are transferred directly, without sale or conversion. The reward is funded by punishment of the leaker, with no independent pool subsidy. The ETH component redirects the existing fee rather than creating a second payment. Multiple represented seats redirect their full combined fee. If the violator has no normal fee, including in a quorum-failed round or following a missing, late or invalid reveal, the ETH component is zero; the applicable utility-token penalty and reward remain separate.

## P06. Premature-disclosure proof period and collateral limits

Source P, lines 323–333.

Raw-excerpt SHA-256: `c9adfd6d42a6bef673c1feae5b32ce90ef3c4e604fecd35e8a1090c9c1e9bbd2`.

> Every successfully drafted initial or appeal round has a fixed proof-opening window after its scheduled reveal deadline and before issuance. Let $t_{\mathrm{reveal}}$ be that deadline and $\Delta_{\mathrm{proof}}>0$ the pool-configured proof-opening duration, frozen with the round timetable before reports are accepted. The proof cutoff is
>
> $$
> t_{\mathrm{proof}}=t_{\mathrm{reveal}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{proof}}.
> $$
>
> The provisional default for $\Delta_{\mathrm{proof}}$ is 48 elapsed hours. It is a design parameter, not an empirically validated safety threshold. The window runs for its full duration even if all curators reveal early or every currently known proof is ready. It is anchored to the scheduled reveal deadline, not the last reveal or a later observer's recognition of closure. Existing admissible proofs opened after the corresponding on-chain reveal but before the reveal deadline remain valid; they need no resubmission. Leak precommits must still be included strictly before the reveal deadline, and the new window does not admit late score reveals or late precommits. Proof openings must be included and validated strictly before $t_{\mathrm{proof}}$, using protocol inclusion time. At or after that cutoff, no new opening is accepted even if a closing transaction has not run. A bare precommit, invalid proof, unresponsive reporter or later submission cannot reset or extend the window.
>
> A valid ruling may be issued only at or after $t_{\mathrm{proof}}$; this permits the transition but does not execute it automatically. The successful reporter is selected from qualifying accepted proofs before fee recipients are assigned. Immediate fee withdrawability and appeal admission begin at actual issuance after this phase. A precommit alone creates no post-issuance claim. Apply the existing earliest-valid-precommit selection rule before assigning the fee recipient. At issuance, the successful reporter replaces the violator as recipient of that fee, at most once for the same violator and round, without changing total $J$, its ceiling or the pool and contest charges. The violator cannot first collect that fee and also leave an entitlement for the reporter. An unclaimed redirected fee stays committed to the reporter outside the backer pot; claiming it does not charge the funding source again. This is a leak-penalty recipient override, not a clawback caused by a later appeal score.
>
> A coordinated leaker and reporter can retain the transferred assets within their coalition; the transfers alone do not establish a net coalition penalty. Using different addresses does not establish independent ownership. A leak penalty may consume only the violator's remaining utility-token collateral for the offending round and currently unlocked utility-token deposits. At enforcement, exclude collateral still locked for every other active round and amounts already confiscated or transferred; count each token once. The requested penalty remains the configured multiplier of the offending round's original locked weight, but actual confiscation is capped by this eligible current balance. A shortfall does not consume other rounds' collateral, create extra debt or require a top-up. Those other rounds keep their collateral and original voting and quorum weights. For example, with 100 tokens locked in the offending round, 100 in another active round and no unlocked deposits, a requested 300-token penalty can confiscate only 100; the other round's 100 remain locked. This rule prohibits removing another round's backing through the leak transfer, while the within-coalition transfer limitation remains. It adds no preventive freeze: withdrawals or new locks permitted by the existing rules may reduce the eligible balance before enforcement. Unclaimed token entitlements are not part of the deposited balance until credited to it. Drafted attempts that fail quorum retain their own round collateral and ordinary-round exclusivity through the same full proof window, then complete the existing cancellation and penalty accounting. They issue no ruling and earn no ETH service fee; valid leak proofs can still receive the actual utility tokens confiscated under the existing cap. Appeal retries retain their level funding reservations and use a fresh round timetable with the same pinned duration. Pre-draw cancellation and an empty draft require no proof window. This rule adds no freeze of otherwise unlocked deposits or other rounds' collateral. The fixed window provides a protocol opportunity to open proof, not a guarantee of network access or transaction inclusion. Other exceptional cancellation rules remain to be specified. No conversion rate, minting, reserve subsidy or token purchase is implied. Utility-token reward rounding follows the rule above; claim execution and numerical representation remain to be specified.

## P07. Issued rulings, pinned revisions and revision-attributed balances

Source P, lines 344–352.

Raw-excerpt SHA-256: `45b696f4e290be0c1ed6cf3b829867b24da457389818e0b198308869bf9f9a7b`.

Displayed-excerpt SHA-256: `45b696f4e290be0c1ed6cf3b829867b24da457389818e0b198308869bf9f9a7b`.

> A ruling is **issued** when a jury completes a valid relevance round. Issuance opens the appeal filing window and makes the earned ETH jury fees immediately withdrawable under the Relevance layer's fee rule; it does not make the ruling final. Technical round finalization means this completion and issuance event. Ruling finality, which governs appeal backer payouts and the curator coherence settlement in step 7, is separate. The effective relevance score follows the adoption rules below while a challenge remains possible.
>
> Every relevance ruling identifies the item and the content revision it assesses. > $$
>
> The ideal payouts sum to the net common pot $Q$. Every positive-distance position has a positive ideal share. A closer position has a larger ideal share; halving the distance doubles its ideal payout relative to a position whose distance is unchanged. Adding another positive-distance position changes the common pot and normalization, but not the ratio between two existing positive-distance ideal payouts at a fixed terminal score. > Escalation is intended to discipline initial reports through the threat of appeal. Because step 7 grades every committee against the chain's terminal adjudicated score, an appeal ruling that moves the score exposes earlier reports far from it to distance-based penalties. ## P14. Architectural commitments and minimal governance

Source P, lines 179–185.

Raw-excerpt SHA-256: `d95c4e489b0d349e1c8e100fdb3a979da18b6c3ae852857ecfbd887a5563d1f5`.

> Three architectural commitments anchor the design:
>
> 1. **Permissionless pools.** Any address may create a pool with a domain, a claim template, an evidence policy, and a relevance policy. If the pool is bad, interfaces and users can ignore it.
> 2. **Immutable contracts.** Protocol changes require new deployments and cross-version aggregation, not in-place upgrades.
> 3. **Minimal governance.** The preference ordering is: eliminating the need for governance is best; good governance is acceptable; bad governance is worse; and no governance at all (ungoverned failure modes) is worst. If a design can eliminate a governance surface, it should.
>
> These commitments limit discretionary control over shared protocol rules. They do not eliminate governance of pool purposes, funding, interfaces, or migration. Here, **trustless** refers to reducing dependence on a single operator through inspectable state and enforceable procedures; readers still depend on evidence producers, adjudicators, and policy choices.

> ## Optimistic accuracy and nomination-scoped outcomes
>
> In the intended design, a factual challenge may begin at any time while the nomination is active, including before its first relevance score and during a relevance assessment or appeal. A withdrawn nomination cannot receive a new challenge. The inspected contracts retain narrower admission conditions; those are implementation evidence, not the intended timing rule. Debunking stops future accrual but preserves all funding earned up to closure as an obligation to that nomination’s beneficiary, including amounts not yet claimed. The nomination bond is forfeited separately; it is not earned funding.
>
> The working paper's Accuracy layer owns this rule: publication requires the normal structural transaction requirements and bond, without prior semantic approval. A successful factual-falsification or non-falsifiability challenge debunks the bonded nomination, forfeits its bond and ends eligibility for future pool funding. Debunking attaches to that nomination, not to a person or all nominations associated with a project. A fresh nomination has its own identity and bond and remains subject to ordinary submission rules; it does not reopen or inherit the earlier nomination's entitlements. The working funding profile no longer permits a reputation threshold to block a fresh nomination solely because an earlier one was debunked. Historical reputation accounting remains separate.
>
> Having necessary material ready for relevance assessment is the nomination's responsibility. The clarification creates no separate pre-draft availability approver and no automatic zero score for inaccessible material. Missing material may establish a violation under the applicable pool policy, but is not by itself proof of falsity. The absence of a successful challenge does not certify either accuracy or evidence availability.
>
> ## Earning, claims and nomination withdrawal
>
> In the intended ongoing-funding design, holdback applies only to new nominations before they begin earning. Later rating increases and decreases change future earning when adopted under the ordinary finalization rules, with no new holdback. Once funding is earned, its beneficiary can claim it immediately, including during an accuracy dispute or after the nomination is debunked. An earning condition must not be described as a lock on an already-earned balance. Appeal-backer pots remain governed by their separate chain-finality rules.
>
> Withdrawing a nomination is a separate operation from claiming its earned funds. A nomination withdrawal requested after an accuracy dispute enters the event queue behind that dispute and cannot execute before it settles. If the dispute ends in debunking, that queued withdrawal is cancelled because the nomination has already closed. If the challenge is rejected, the queued exit may proceed under the ordinary exit rules. The inspected contracts' older claim-freeze behavior is retained below as implementation evidence, not as the intended rule.
>
> ## Score-to-funding interpretation
>
> The intended application first curates policy-relative knowledge about what matters, represented by relative allocation weights. Curation costs require funding. A beneficiary distribution budget is useful upfront but optional. For effective weights $w_i=\min(\text{requested weight}_i,\text{adopted score}_i)$ among eligible nominations and positive total $W=\sum_iw_i$, the normalized vector has entries $p_i=w_i/W$ and sums to one. A distribution budget $B$ can arrive later and allocate $B p_i$ to each nomination; a funding-flow rate uses the same shares. A zero distribution budget leaves this vector unchanged and creates no beneficiary earnings. Zero total eligible weight leaves no allocatable vector; it does not authorize an equal split. Curation funding retains the existing nomination-bond, jury-service-fee and dispute or appeal rules. This is the author's current design clarification, not a claim established by the historical implementation sources below.
>
> The working paper's Relevance layer owns the interpretation of curated relevance as relative funding weights when an application uses direct proportional allocation. The numeric ratio is a chosen allocation policy, not a measured ratio of public benefit, causal impact or the value of another grant. Each pool's curation policy also decides whether funding need or expected benefit from another grant should affect relevance. The mechanism imposes neither objective and adds no separate need adjustment to the resulting score. Each pool creator authors the rubric and its intended numeric meaning; there is no universal curation policy or new transformation setting.
>
> The inspected funding implementation caps the curated score by the requested weight before normalizing eligible earning weights. The worked example retains that calculation. The working paper's retroactive public goods funding profile instead normalizes scores across surviving nominations under its existing eligibility and holdback rules. The Truth Post feed's relevance-and-confidence ranking, curator voting weights and inverse-distance appeal payouts remain separate. Explaining the funding ratios does not validate the underlying assessments or the allocation's outcomes.
>
> The master paper also makes full allocation with positive eligible weight intentional. A sole positive eligible weight receives the entire distributable amount, even if its score is low; request caps limit relative weight, not absolute payout. This is an intended invitation for other nominations to compete for a share. It does not guarantee entry or independent competition, and it changes no admission window, eligibility condition, holdback, reserve or zero-weight rule.
