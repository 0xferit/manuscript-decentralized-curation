# Chomsky & Herman (1988): Manufacturing Consent

**Citation**: Chomsky, N., & Herman, E. S. (1988). *Manufacturing Consent: The Political Economy of the Mass Media*. Pantheon Books.

## Core thesis

Mass media in democratic societies function as a propaganda system, not through overt censorship but through structural filters that shape what becomes "news." Five filters determine media output: (1) ownership concentration, (2) advertising as primary revenue, (3) sourcing from official channels, (4) "flak" (organized pressure), and (5) ideological framing. The result is manufactured consent: the public believes it is freely informed while the information pipeline is structurally biased.

## Key concepts

- **Propaganda model**: the five filters are structural, not conspiratorial. No individual needs to be dishonest; the system selects for outputs that serve the interests of owners, advertisers, and official sources.
- **Manufactured consent**: the public's beliefs are shaped by the information they receive, and the information they receive is shaped by structural incentives. Consent is not freely given; it is manufactured by the information pipeline.
- **Structural vs. intentional bias**: the key insight is that you do not need a conspiracy. Economic incentives, source dependencies, and organizational pressures produce biased output as a natural consequence of the system's structure.

## Connection to our paper

We cite Chomsky/Herman for one specific argument in the "Why this matters" section:

- **Weaponization as a failure mode**: "when one entity controls what becomes 'known,' the distance between curation and propaganda collapses to a question of intent." This is the Chomsky/Herman thesis expressed in our framework.
- **Incompetence vs. weaponization are indistinguishable**: from the consumer's perspective, you cannot tell whether your feed is poorly curated or deliberately curated against your interests. Chomsky/Herman demonstrate the structural version; we generalize it as an inherent property of centralized curation.
- **Advertising filter**: their second filter (advertising as primary revenue) maps directly to our advertising instantiation. The current system has advertisers paying platforms regardless of truth; our mechanism makes truthful advertising cheaper than false advertising.

## Limitations relevant to us

- Chomsky/Herman analyze mass media in specific political-economic contexts (US, post-WWII). The model may not transfer cleanly to decentralized, pseudonymous information systems.
- The propaganda model is primarily descriptive and critical. It does not propose mechanisms for fixing the problem, which is where our paper picks up.
- Some critics argue the model is too structural and underestimates the agency of journalists, editors, and consumers. We do not need to defend the model in full; we cite it for the structural insight about centralized control.
